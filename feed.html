AI rewards judgment, not effort

There's a story the industry keeps telling itself: AI is levelling the playing field. Junior designers can generate ten directions in the time it takes a senior designer to sketch two. Anyone with a good prompt can produce something that looks polished, considered, designed.

That part is true. The conclusion people draw from it — that experience matters less now — is wrong.

I've been using AI tools in my daily design process for a while: Claude Code for prototyping logic, Cursor for browser-native work, generative tools for rapid concept exploration. What I've found, consistently, is that AI doesn't replace design judgment. It amplifies it.

When I use AI to pressure-test a flow against edge cases, it surfaces problems I might have caught in a third review cycle — except I catch them before engineering sees the design. That's not automation. That's judgment applied earlier and cheaper.

When I generate ten directions before committing to one, I'm not abdicating decision-making. I'm widening the search space before I narrow it. The skill is still knowing which of the ten is worth developing — and why the other nine, however polished they look, are wrong for this specific problem, this specific user, this specific set of constraints.

That knowledge is built slowly. It comes from shipping things, watching them fail, understanding why, and adjusting. Twelve years in high-stakes domains — financial trading platforms, global HR infrastructure, live EdTech — gives you a library of patterns and failure modes that no prompt can generate on your behalf.

Junior designers using AI produce more output. That's real. Experienced designers using AI make better decisions faster. The difference is what you're multiplying.

I'm not anxious about AI. I use it every day and it's made me more effective than I've ever been. But I think the designers treating it as a shortcut to senior-level work are going to be disappointed. And the companies using it as a reason to hire less experienced people are going to pay for that in ways they won't immediately see.

AI is a force multiplier. What it multiplies is up to you.
